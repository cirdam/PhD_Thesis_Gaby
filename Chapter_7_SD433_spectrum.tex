\chapter{The SD-433 Energy spectrum}
\label{chapter:SD433_Spectrum}
\label{chap:sdpectrum}
\selectlanguage{english}

With the developed energy reconstruction from the previous chapter and the proper determination of the full efficiency to have a proper estimation of the exposure, this chapter focuses on the measuerment of the cosmic ray spectrum with the \sd{433}.
For this, \cref{sec:raw_spectrum} is devoted to the construction of the raw spectrum. 
After this, the forward-folding formalism is presented in order to fit the spectrum and properly measure the second knee in the cosmic ray spectrum.
Several checks were developed to assess the robustnes of the method and to understand the impact of the different detector characteristics.
In \cref{sec:unfolded_spectrum}, the unfolded spectrum is shown and the robustness of the measurement of the second knee is studied.
Finally, in ~\cref{sec:systematics} the impact of the different sources of systematics is assessed to give a complete picture of the measuerment of the spectrum and the second knee.

%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
\section{Raw spectrum}
\label{sec:raw_spectrum}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
With the recorded events with the \sd{433} between July 1 2018 and December 21 2021 the cosmic ray flux can start to be estimated.
A histogram of the energy of the events, allows to estimate the raw flux of cosmic rays,
\begin{align}
    J_{i}^\text{raw}(E)=\frac{N_i}{\varepsilon\Delta E_i},
\end{align}
where $N_i$ is the number of events in energy bin $i$, $\Delta E_i$ is the width of the energy bin and $\varepsilon$ is the exposure. 
As determined in \cref{sec:exposure}, the exposure, $\varepsilon$, is \SDFourThreeThreeExposure and is independent of energy over the range considered here.
~\cref{fig:raw_spectrum} shows the raw spectrum together with the $1-\sigma$ Poisson confidence interval. The number at each datapoint show the number of events in that bin.
\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.75\linewidth]{figures/chapter_spectrum/sd433_raw_spectrum.pdf}
    \caption{Raw cosmic ray flux measured with the \sd{433}. The datapoints show the raw flux.
    The uncertainty bars represent the $1-\sigma$ Poisson confidence interval. The number of events is also shown in text for each bin.}
    \label{fig:raw_spectrum}
\end{figure}
The cosmic-ray flux spans many decades in energy, roughly following power law with a spectral index $\gamma\sim3$.
Because of this, it is usual for the flux to be shown multiplied by $E^3$ to highlight the different features where $\gamma$ changes.
\cref{fig:raw_spectrum} shows the \sd{433} spectrum multiplied by $E^3$, where a change in the slope of the flux around $\lg(E/\text{eV})\sim17.3$ giving a hint of the presence of the second knee.

\section{Forward folding}
\label{forward_folding}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%
To properly characterize the features of the spectrum, the influence of the detector in the measurement has to be taken into account.
Many experiments resource to an unfolding procedure ~\cite{Book:Cowan:Statistics}.
However, this methods need an external regularization to be included for the statistical fluctuations not being catastrophically increased.
Because of this, the Pierre Auger Collaboration instead relies on a forward folding procedure to \textit{fold} the model with the detector and match the data.
This method has the advantage of being self regularized.
To do this, the expected number of events for a given spectrum model, $J(E)$, is folded via a migration matrix that takes into account the detector response.
The response matrix of the detector is built to fold the response in the \textit{true energy space}, $j$,  into de \textit{detector energy space}, $i$,
\begin{align}
    R_{ij}=\frac{
    \int\limits_{\Delta E_i}\text{d}E_i
    \int\limits_{\Delta E_j}\text{d}E_j\,J(E_j)\epsilon(E_j)\kappa(E_i|E_j,\sigma,b)
    }{
    \int\limits_{\Delta E_j}\text{d}E_j\,J(E_j)
    }
    \label{eq:migration_matrix}
\end{align}
where $\kappa(E_i))|E_j,\sigma,b)$ is the response function of the detector.
Using the resolution, $\sigma(E)$, and bias, $b(E)$ from ~\cref{chap:energy_reconstruction}, the resolution function of the \gls{sd} is modelled as a Gaussian distribution,
\begin{align}
    \kappa(E_i \mid E_j,\sigma,b)
    &=
    \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\sigma(E_j)}
    \exp\left[
        -\frac{
            \left(E_i-E_j\left[1+b(E_j)\right]\right)^2
        }{
            2\sigma^2(E_j)
        }
    \right].
\label{eq:resolution_function}
\end{align}
\noindent with mean value $E_j(1+b(E))$ and standard deviation $\sigma(E)$.
Given that the bias vanishes as the energy increases, the response function is dominated by the resolution of the detector.

When the detector is fully efficient, the migration matrix reduces to the probability of an event with true energy $E_j$ to be detected in the bin with energy $E_i$.
At the highest energies, the relative resolution is dominated by the shower-to-shower fluctuations, and the matrix becomes diagonal. %Find better wording because it's not exactly diagonal
From a spectrum model with a set of free parameters $\mathbf{s}$, the expected number of events on a bin,
\begin{align}
\mu=\int\limits_{\Delta E}\text{d}E\,J(E|\mathbf{s}),
\end{align}
Via the migration matrix this expected number of events can be folded by the detector response,
\begin{align}
\nu_i=\sum\limits_jR_{ij}\mu_j.
\end{align}
To estimate the set of parameters, $\mathbf{s}$, a Poisson likelihood fit can be performed. 
For each bin in the reconstructed energy space, the log-Poisson likelihood is defined,
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{L}_i=\ln(\psi_i^2/\psi^2_{min})=-2\left[(\nu_i-n_i)-n_i\ln(\nu_i/n_i)\right],
\end{align*}
where $n_i$ is the reconstructed number of events on the energy bin $i$.
By varying the set of free parameters, $\mathbf{s}$, the combined likelihood, $\mathcal{L}=\sum\limits_i\mathcal{L_i}$, is minimized.
This method presents the challenge that the migration matrix depends explicitly on the spectrum model that is used, so it has to be recalculated at each step.
However, given that the spectrum falls sharply as $\sim E^3$ of the energy, if the binning in the true space is fine enough, the response function and efficiency can be considered constant within each bin and removed from the integral.
In this way, the dependence of $J(E|\mathbf{s})$ is the same in the numerator and denominator of \cref{eq:migration_matrix} and the explicit dependence vanishes.
Then, the approximated migration matrix simplifies to
\begin{align}
    R_{ij}=\int\limits_{\Delta E_ij}\text{d}E_i\,\kappa(E_i|E_j,\sigma,b)\epsilon(E_j).
    \label{eq:matrix_approx}
\end{align}

In order to test the robustness of this approximation, a the binning in the true energy space is chosen as $\Delta\lg(E_j/\text{eV})=0.01$ while the binning in the reconstructed energy space is chosen as $\Delta\lg(E_i/\text{eV})=0.1$ to be compatible with the resolution of the detector.

The approximated migration matrix for the \sd{433} is shown in \cref{fig:migration_matrix} together with the full efficiency threshold represented by the red dashed line.
\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.75\linewidth]{figures/chapter_spectrum/sd433_migration_matrix.pdf}
    \caption{Migration matrix for the \sd{433}. The red dashed line indicates the full efficiency threshold. The identity is also shown in white}
    \label{fig:migration_matrix}
\end{figure}
At the lowest energies, since the resolution is lower, contributions from further bins become more relevant.
To avoid border effects, the migration matrix is calculated for energies below full efficiency. 
The probability of events not being detected is also compensated with the efficiency parametrization from \cref{chap:sd433}.
However, the contribution from events below full efficiency comes approximately only from two bins below. %improve wording.
At the highest energies, the matrix becomes independent of the energy due to the dominance of the shower-to-shower fluctuations in the resolution.

To assess the impact of using the approximated matrix, a power-law spectrum was assumed with a slope $\gamma=-3$, $J(E)=J_0E^{\gamma}$.
The expected number of folded events, $\nu_i$, was calculated using both migration matrices, $R^{\text{approx}}_{ij}$ and $R^{\text{exact}}_{ij}$.
\cref{fig:nu_ratio} shows the relative difference between the expected number of events calculated with the approximate and exact methods.
The difference between the two is negligible in the whole energy range.
For this reason, the approximated method is chosen for the forward-folding of the spectrum to avoid making a priori assumptions for the spectrum shape and parameters, and recalculating the matrix in each iteration of the fit.

\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.75\linewidth]{figures/chapter_spectrum/nu_ratio.pdf}
    \caption{Percent difference between the folded number of events using the migration matrix from \cref{eq:migration_matrix} and the approximated matrix \cref{eq:matrix_approx}.}
    \label{fig:nu_ratio}
\end{figure}

Once the set of best fit parameters is determined, the correction factors,
\begin{align}
    c_i=\mu_i/\nu_i,
    \label{eq:correction_factor}
\end{align}
 are used to estimate the unfolded cosmic ray flux, $J_i=c_iJ_{\text{raw}}$.

The correction factors obtained from the fit to the measured \sd{433} dataset are shown in \cref{fig:correction_factors}.
The correction is largest close to the full-efficiency threshold and becomes approximately constant at the highest energies, where the relative energy resolution is dominated by shower-to-shower fluctuations.
\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.75\linewidth]{figures/chapter_spectrum/sd433_nominal_correction_factors.pdf}
    \caption{Correction factors obtained from the forward-folding fit to the measured \sd{433} dataset.}
    \label{fig:correction_factors}
\end{figure}

\section{The \sd{433} Unfolded spectrum}
\label{sec:unfolded_spectrum}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

With the developed forward-folding fit and unfolding, the \sd{433} spectrum can be then measured.
Given the low statistics at the highest energy range from the spectrum, the maximum energy used for the fit was $\lg(E\text{eV})=18.5$, just below the next spectral feature, the ankle.

In order to test the presence of a knee-like structure in the spectrum, a simple power law spectrum was performed,
\begin{align}
    J(E)=J_0(E)^{-\gamma},
\end{align}
where the normalization constant, $J_0$, and spectral index $\gamma$ where left as free parameters.
The obtained spectrum is shown in the supplementary material in \cref{app:chap_spectrum}.
However, with a Poisson deviance of $D^2/\text{dof}=\SDFourThreeThreePowerLawDeviance/\SDFourThreeThreePowerLawDof$, the simple-power-law hypothesis is rejected with a $p$-value of $\SDFourThreeThreePowerLawPValue$.
This is in accordance with the result published in Ref. ~\cite{PierreAuger:2021InfillSpectrum}, where a soft turn-down of the spectrum is seen but the second knee cannot be charaterized.
As a consequence, a broken power law with a soft transition is used,
\begin{align}
    J(E)=J_0\left(
    \frac{E}{10^{17}\,\text{eV}}
    \right)^{-\gamma_0}
    \left[
    1+\left(
    \frac{E}{E_{01}}
    \right)^{\frac{1}{\omega_{01}}}
    \right]^{(\gamma_0-\gamma_1)\omega_{01}},
\label{eq:bplwst}
\end{align}
where $E_{01}$ is the energy of the second knee, $\gamma_0$ and $\gamma_1$ are the spectral indices before and after the feature and, $\omega_{01}$ is a parameter that governs the width of the transition ~\cite{PierreAuger:2021InfillSpectrum}.

When fitting the spectrum data for the different dataset, it was observed that the $\omega_{01}$ could not be properly constrained.
To study this, 300 iterations of toy Monte-Carlo spectra were simulated.
Using \cref{eq:bplwst} sets of 40,000 events, in approximation with the number of events from the measured spectrum, were generated weighted by the detection efficiency model.
Then, each event was smeared by the Gaussian resolution function from \cref{eq:resolution_function}.
Finally, the fit using the migration matrix from \cref{eq:matrix_approx} was performed.

From the 300 iterations, 296 were successfully fitted.
However, 125 yielded $\omega_{01}$ values at the limit of the fit.
A histogram of of the $\omega_{01}$ values is shown in \cref{fig:omega_monte_carlo} and the rest of the parameters can be seen in the supplementary material \cref{appendix_chapter_spectrum}.
The piled up distribution of the fitted values show that with the statistics present for the current \sd{433} measurement, the parameter $\omega_{01}$ cannot be properly constrained.

\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/chapter_spectrum/omega_free_toy_histograms.pdf}
    \caption{Distributions of the spectral parameters reconstructed from toy Monte-Carlo spectra when the transition width $\omega_{01}$ is left free. The vertical dotted lines indicate the injected values. The accumulation of fits at the allowed boundaries shows that $\omega_{01}$ cannot be reliably constrained with the present \sd{433} statistics.}
    \label{fig:omega_monte_carlo}
\end{figure}

To assess the impact of omega, a set of 10,000 spectra of 40,000 events were generated using a $\omega_{01}=0.25$ (a soft transition) and fitted using a fixed value of 0.05 (hard transition).
Histograms of the fitted $\lg(E_{01})$, $\gamma_0$ and $\gamma_1$ are shown in \cref{fig:omega_wrong} together with its median and the injected values.
The result from the simulations show that the impact of using a wrong $\omega_{01}$ value in the fit is within the statistical uncertainty of the fit.
Because of this, a fixed $\omega_{01}$ of 0.05 is chosen, leaving this parameter to be properly constrained in \cref{chap:combined_spectrum} when including the much greater statistic of the \sd{750} in the bins around the second knee.

\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/chapter_spectrum/omega_wrong_closure.png}
    \caption{Distributions of the fitted spectral parameters for toy spectra generated with $\omega_{01}=0.25$ and fitted with the transition width fixed to $\omega_{01}=0.05$. The vertical dotted lines indicate the injected values.}
    \label{fig:omega_wrong}
\end{figure}

With the fixed value of the transition width, the forward-folded fit was performed once more, obtaining an energy of \secondKneeEnergy, \gammaZero and \gammaOne.

The resulting unfolded spectrum and the residuals with respect to the fitted model are shown in \cref{fig:sd433_unfolded_spectrum}.
\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{figures/chapter_spectrum/sd433_unfolded_spectrum_with_residuals.pdf}
    \caption{Unfolded cosmic-ray spectrum measured with the \sd{433} and the best-fit broken power law with a smooth transition. The lower panel shows the residuals with respect to the fitted model.}
    \label{fig:sd433_unfolded_spectrum}
\end{figure}

To assess the stability of the second knee position, the fit was repeated increasing the minimum energy.
The result is consistent up to $\lg(E_{\text{min}}/\mathrm{eV})=17.2$ when the quality of the fit is greatly degraded. The results for the spectral parameters can be found in \cref{appendix_chapter_spectrum}.

\section{Spectrum systematics}
\label{sec:spectrum_systematics}
%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%%

To understand the robustness of the measurement of the Second Knee, the different sources of systematics were evaluated using the forward-folding machinery deveolped in the last section.
The main source of systematic uncertainty arises from the $\sim14\%$ energy systematic inherited from the \sd{750} calibration using the FD.
Other sources are included in the assumptions in the detector behaviour used in the construction of the migration matrix, such as the primary composition used in the bias, efficiency and resolution and the systematic uncertainty in the calibration parameters from \cref{chapter:reconstruction}.
These different sources were treated as uncorrelated and the impact in both the flux and the spectral parameters was studied.
After that the sum of the different contributions allow to construct the proper systematic uncertainty in the measurement.
Also, the systematic uncertainty from the $\omega_{01}$ assumption is studied in order to see the impact in the second knee.

\subsection{FD energy systematic}

The impact of the FD energy scale can be treated using forward-folding and can also be derived analytically.
Assuming a change in  energy, $E'=(1+\delta)E$ where $\delta=\pm0.14$, the change in the flux can be assessed using the change of variable theorem for a distribution,
\begin{align}
    J'(E')=J(E)\left|\frac{dE}{dE'}\right|
    \label{eq:change_of_variables}
\end{align}
where $J(E)$ and $J'(E')$ are the flux measured on the $E$ and $E'$ energy scales respectively.
Given a power-law spectrum, $J(E)=J_0E^{-\gamma}$, the relative change in the spectrum becomes
\begin{align}
\frac{\Delta J}{J}=(1+\delta)^{\gamma-1}-1,
\label{eq:energy_systematic_pl}
\end{align}
which gives flux shifts of approximately $\SI{+36}{\percent}$ and $\SI{-30}{\percent}$ for $\gamma=3.35$ and $\delta=+0.14$ and $-0.14$, respectively.
The same derivation can be done for \cref{eq:bplwst}, giving
\begin{align}
\frac{\Delta J(E)}{J(E)}
=
\frac{1}{1+\delta}
\frac{
J\left(\frac{E}{1+\delta}\right)
}{
J(E)
}
-1
\label{eq:energy_systematic_bplwst}
\end{align}
From the fitted \sd{433} flux this estimation is shown in brown in \cref{fig:energy_systematic} together with the limits derived from \cref{eq:energy_systematic_pl}.
Also, the impact of each systematic uncertainty is evaluated by repeating the complete forward-folding and unfolding when shifting coherently the energy of each event by $\delta$.
The migration matrix is left unchanged because this variation does not modify the detector resolution, bias, or efficiency.
The unfolded spectra for the events changed in energy is shown in \cref{appendix_chapter_spectrum}.

The resulting changes in the flux are also shown in \cref{fig:energy_systematic}.
Both results agree well at maximum and minimum energies.
However, in the region near the second knee the results differ due to the uncertainty in $\gamma_{01}$ originated by the low statistics at the highest energies.

\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{figures/chapter_spectrum/delta_j_over_j.pdf}
    \caption{Relative change in the fitted flux after coherently shifting the reconstructed energies by $+14\%$ and $-14\%$. The solid curves show the result of repeating the forward-folding fit, while the dash-dotted curves show the analytical expectation from \cref{eq:energy_systematic_bplwst}. The horizontal dashed lines indicate the corresponding asymptotic simple-power-law expectations.}
    \label{fig:energy_systematic}
\end{figure}

The effect of the energy-scale shifts on the fitted spectral parameters is shown in \cref{fig:energy_systematic_fit_parameters}.
\begin{figure}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/chapter_spectrum/energy_systematic_fit_parameters.pdf}
    \caption{Fitted normalization $J_0$, second-knee energy $E_{01}$, and spectral indices $\gamma_0$ and $\gamma_1$ for the nominal energy scale and for coherent shifts of $+14\%$ and $-14\%$. The error bars show the statistical uncertainties of the fits. The dashed lines in the upper panels indicate the expectations obtained by directly propagating the energy-scale shift.}

The changes in $\gamma_0$ and $\gamma_1$ are consistent with statistical uncertainties, in accordance with what is expected, given that a uniform change in energy does not impact the slope of the spectrum.
However, the change in $E_{01}$ is larger than the one expected from \cref{eq:energy_systematic_bplwst}.
To be conservative, the systematic uncertainty assigned, $\Delta \lg (E_{01}/\text{eV})=0.10$, is the highest one coming from the unfolding procedure.


\subsection{Response function systematics}
\label{subsec:response_systematics}

To study the impact in the spectrum of the assumptions made for the response function defined in \cref{eq:resolution_function}, different changes in the bias, resolution and efficiency.
The contribution of the uncertainty in the energy resolution was evaluated by varying the parameters of the resolution model defined in \cref{eq:resolution_sigma}, following the procedure of Ref.~\cite{PierreAuger:2021Iifill_spectrum}.
The parameters $R_{\text{sh}}$ and $R_0$ were shifted by their $\pm1\sigma$ uncertainties, corresponding to variations of $\pm0.01$ respectively.
For each variation, the migration matrix was recalculated keeping all other components of the response function unchanged and the spectrum was fitted.
The spectral-shape parameters remain well within their nominal statistical uncertainties. 
For each source of systematic uncertainty, the contribution to $\Delta J/J$ was calculated as the root mean square of the relative flux variations.
The resulting contribution from the energy resolution is shown in the left panel of \cref{fig:systematic_budget}.
Its magnitude is largest near the full-efficiency threshold, where it reaches approximately $1.1\%$, and decreases with energy.
Increasing the resolution parameters lowers the reconstructed flux, whereas decreasing them raises it.  
The resulting spectra and fitted parameters are reported in \cref{appendix_chapter_spectrum}.
    

The dependence of the detector response on the assumed mass composition was estimated using the bias and efficiency parametrizations obtained in \cref{sec} for pure proton and pure iron primaries.
Each component was evaluated separately, following the same procedure used for the resolution systematic.

The bias variations leave the position of the Second Knee and the spectral indices essentially unchanged.
However, the corresponding contribution to $\Delta J/J$ reaches approximately $4.3\%$ near the full-efficiency threshold.

The composition dependence of the efficiency produces a smaller contribution to the flux systematic, with a maximum of $\sim1.5\%$.
Nevertheless, it shifts the position of the Second Knee downward by approximately $\Delta\lg(E_{01}/\text{eV})=-0.03$.

The contribution to the total flux systematic for both the bias and efficiency mass composition is also shown in the left panel of \cref{fig:systematic_budget}.

\subsection{Calibration systematic}
\label{subsec:calibration_systematic}

The energy assigned to each \sd{433} event is obtained from the calibration with the \sd{750} energy estimator described in \cref{sec:calibration}.
In addition to the systematic uncertainty inherited from the FD energy scale, the finite precision of this calibration introduces an independent contribution to the spectrum systematic.
This contribution is associated with the uncertainties in the normalization $A$ and slope $B$ of the calibration relation.
Unlike a uniform shift of the energy scale, such as the one introduced by the uncertainty in $A$, a variation of the slope $B$ produces an energy-dependent change in the reconstructed energies and can therefore modify the spectral indices.

The effect of the calibration slope can be understood analytically.
For the calibration relation
\begin{align}
    E=A\left(\frac{S_{30}}{S_0}\right)^B,
\end{align}
a change from $B$ to $B'$, while keeping $A$ fixed, transforms the reconstructed energy according to
\begin{align}
    E'
    =
    A
    \left(\frac{E}{A}\right)^{\frac{B'}{B}}.
    \label{eq:calibration_energy_transformation}
\end{align}
The corresponding transformation of the flux follows from the change of variables in \cref{eq:change_of_variables},
\begin{align}
    J'(E')
    =
    \frac{B}{B'}
    \frac{E}{E'}
    J(E).
    \label{eq:calibration_flux_transformation}
\end{align}
For a power-law spectrum, $J(E)\propto E^{-\gamma}$, the transformed spectrum is also a power law with spectral index
\begin{align}
    \gamma'
    =
    1+(\gamma-1)\frac{B}{B'}.
    \label{eq:calibration_spectral_index}
\end{align}
The effective change in the spectral index is therefore
\begin{align}
    \Delta\gamma
    =
    (\gamma-1)
    \left(\frac{B}{B'}-1\right).
    \label{eq:calibration_spectral_index_change}
\end{align}
Consequently, the uncertainty in $A$ primarily changes the energy and flux normalization, whereas the uncertainty in $B$ modifies the shape of the reconstructed spectrum.

In the systematic evaluation, the calibration parameters were varied according to their systematic uncertainties from \cref{sec:energy_calibration}.
The nominal values $(A,B)=(119.5~\mathrm{PeV},0.946)$ were replaced by $(A^+,B^-)=(125.7~\mathrm{PeV},0.909)$ and $(A^-,B^+)=(113.3~\mathrm{PeV},0.983)$.
The calibration curves for the detector are shown in the left panel of \cref{fig:calibration_systematic}.
For each variation, the energy of every event was recalculated and the spectrum was fitted again.
The fitted spectra are shown on the right panel of \cref{fig:calibration_systematic}.
\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/chapter_spectrum/calibration_systematic.pdf}
    \caption{
        Effect of the calibration systematic on the reconstructed spectrum.
        The left panel shows the reference calibration relation together with the lower- and upper-slope variations.
        The right panel shows the corresponding unfolded spectra multiplied by $E^3$, with the lines representing the fitted spectral functions.
    }
    \label{fig:calibration_systematic}
\end{figure}
Using the values from the fit in \cref{sec:unfolded_spectrum}, $\gamma_0=3.036$ and $\gamma_1=3.342$, \cref{eq:calibration_spectral_index_change} yield $\Delta\gamma_0^\text{analytical}=0.083$ and $\Delta\gamma_1^\text{analytical}=0.095$.
The corresponding changes obtained from the spectrum fit are $\Delta\gamma_0^\text{fit}=0.073$ and$\Delta\gamma_1^\text{fit}=0.081$.
The fitted changes, therefore, agree closely with the analytical expectations.
To be conservative, the largest analytical values are used.
The position of the Second Knee changes by at most $\Delta\lg(E_{01}/\mathrm{eV})=0.024$, well within its nominal statistical uncertainty.
The change in the flux produced by the calibration variations is shown in \cref{fig:systematic_budget}.
This source of systematic uncertainty is the second-largest contribution after the energy-scale uncertainty.
Its effect is largest at the boundaries of the fitted energy range because of the increasing event-by-event energy differences, reaching $18.6%$ at $\lg(E/\mathrm{eV})=16.8$ and $13.4%$ at $\lg(E/\mathrm{eV})=18.5$.
