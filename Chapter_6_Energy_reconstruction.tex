\chapter{Energy reconstruction}
\label{chap:energy_reconstruction}
\label{chapter:reconstruction}

This chapter describes the reconstruction chain that connects the station-level measurements of an event to an estimate of the primary cosmic-ray energy.
In \cref{sec:reconstruction_shower_size}, the \Offline reconstruction is used to determine the shower geometry and to estimate the signal at a reference distance from the shower axis through a fit of the \gls{ldf}.
The zenith-angle dependence caused by atmospheric attenuation is then determined and removed in \cref{sec:cic}.
Finally, in \cref{sec:calibration}, the corrected shower size is calibrated against the energy estimator of the \sd{750} to assign an energy to each \sd{433} event.

\section{Reconstruction of the shower size}
\label{sec:reconstruction_shower_size}

The shower reconstruction proceeds in two stages.
First, the relative start times of the signals measured by the participating stations are used to determine the arrival direction of the shower and the impact position of its core on the ground.
The recorded signals are then fitted as a function of the perpendicular distance to the shower axis using an empirical \gls{ldf}.
The value of this function at the reference distance of \SI{300}{\m}, denoted by $S(300)$, provides the ground-level shower-size estimator used for the \sd{433}.
Because the core position affects the station distances entering the \gls{ldf}, the geometry and shower size are finally determined in a combined fit initialized with the results of the separate reconstructions.
A detailed description of the event-reconstruction procedure used for the surface detector can be found in Ref.~\cite{PierreAuger:2020:SDReconstruction}.
Here, the main elements relevant to the reconstruction of the \sd{433} events are summarized, with particular emphasis on the steps implemented for the present analysis.

\subsection{Geometrical reconstruction}
\label{subsec:geometry_ldf}

The geometrical reconstruction uses the positions and signal start times of the detectors participating in the event.
Its purpose is to determine the position of the shower core on the ground, $\vec{x}_{\mathrm{c}}$, and the unit vector along the shower axis, $\hat{a}$, whose components are the direction cosines from which the zenith angle, $\theta$, and the azimuth angle, $\phi$, are obtained.

The reconstruction begins with a plane-front fit to the station start times, which provides an initial estimate of the arrival direction.
The signal-weighted barycentre of the participating stations is used as the initial estimate of the core position.
These estimates are subsequently refined using a curved description of the shower front.

In the \Offline reconstruction, the most advanced particles of the shower, which define the extreme shower front, are approximated by a sphere expanding at the speed of light from a virtual origin $\vec{x}_0$ at time $t_0$.
The predicted time at which this front reaches a position $\vec{x}$ is
\begin{align}
    c\,t_\mathrm{sh}(\vec{x}) = c\,t_0 + \lvert \vec{x}-\vec{x}_0 \rvert .
    \label{eq:spherical_shower_front}
\end{align}
The fit minimizes the squared differences between the measured and predicted start times,
\begin{align}
    \chi^2 = \sum_i \frac{\left[t_i-t_\mathrm{sh}(\vec{x}_i)\right]^2}{\sigma_{t_i}^2}.
    \label{eq:geometry_chi2}
\end{align}
where $t_i$ and $\sigma_{t_i}$ are the start time measured by station $i$ and its uncertainty, respectively.

The core position is refined together with the lateral distribution of the station signals.
Once $\vec{x}_{\mathrm{c}}$ is determined, the reconstructed shower direction is given by
\begin{align}
    \hat{a}
    =
    \frac{\vec{x}_{0}-\vec{x}_{\mathrm{c}}}
    {\left|\vec{x}_{0}-\vec{x}_{\mathrm{c}}\right|}.
    \label{eq:shower_axis}
\end{align}
The radius of curvature at the core position is
\begin{align}
    R_{0}=\left|\vec{x}_{0}-\vec{x}_{\mathrm{c}}\right|.
\end{align}

For events with at least five participating stations, the radius of curvature is determined from the timing fit.
For events with lower station multiplicity, the number of degrees of freedom is insufficient to constrain the curvature, and $R_{0}$ is fixed according to a parametrization obtained from higher-multiplicity events.

The left panel of \cref{fig:sd433_event_footprint_ldf} illustrates the geometrical reconstruction of an \sd{433} event.
The marker size is proportional to the station signal, while the colour scale represents its start time, progressing from earlier signals in blue to later signals in green.
The black line indicates the reconstructed arrival direction projected onto the ground, and the cross marks the reconstructed core position.
\subsection{Lateral-distribution function}
\label{subsec:ldf}

The shower size is then estimated by modeling the fall-off of the signal $S(r)$ with the distance $r$ to the shower axis using a \gls{ldf} ~\cite{Silli:2021:PerformanceSD433}.
Intrinsic shower-to-shower fluctuations produce variations in the longitudinal development, such that individual events may be sampled at different stages of their development.
As a consequence, the \gls{ldf} exhibits variations on an event-by-event basis, preventing the reconstruction of all the parameters in the shower. 
Instead, the reconstruction is based on an \textit{average} LDF, $\langle S(r)\rangle$, and the expected signal at a fixed reference distance, $r_\text{opt}$, is adopted as the shower size.
The optimal distance of \qty{300}{m} for the \sd{433} is determined by fitting the average LDF and identifying the distance at which the reconstructed shower size is minimally dependent on the LDF slope, following the method described in Ref.~\cite{Newton:2007:OptimalDistance}.
The dependence of the optimal distance on the assumed LDF shape, the primary energy, and the zenith angle remains at approximately the 5\% level~\cite{PierreAuger:2020:SDReconstruction}.
The average LDF is described using an empirically-chosen model based on a modified \gls{nkg}function,
\begin{align}
  \langle S(r)\rangle =
    S(r_\text{opt})
    \left(\frac{r}{r_\text{opt}}\right)^\beta
    \left(\frac{r + r_\text{scale}}{r_\text{opt} + r_\text{scale}}\right)^\beta,
\end{align}
%
where $\beta$ is the LDF slope and $r_\text{scale}=700\,\text{m}$ is a scale parameter.

The slope of the \gls{ldf} was determined on an event-by-event basis using a set of high-quality events with at least five triggered stations, for which $\beta$ could be left as a free parameter in the fit~\cite{Silli:2021:PerformanceSD433}.
The fitted values were then used to parametrize the average dependence of the slope on the shower size and zenith angle as
\begin{align}
    \beta(\theta,y)=a(\theta)+b(\theta)y
\end{align}
where $y=\lg[S(300)/\text{VEM}]$.
The coefficients that govern the dependence in zenith angle, $a(\theta)$ and $b(\theta)$ are modeled as second degree polynomials in $\sec\theta$,
\begin{align}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        a \\
        b
    \end{pmatrix}
    =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        -3.72 & 1.30  & 0.055 \\
         0.98 & -1.30 & 0.385
    \end{pmatrix}
    \begin{pmatrix}
        1 \\
        \sec\theta \\
        \sec^{2}\theta
    \end{pmatrix}.
    \label{eq:beta_parameterization}
\end{align}
The parametrization describes the event-by-event fitted slopes with an average relative difference of approximately $2\%$ over the considered range of shower sizes and zenith angles~\cite{Silli:2021:PerformanceSD433}.
Once determined, this parametrization was fixed in the reconstruction of the complete data set, allowing events with insufficient station multiplicity to constrain the LDF slope to be reconstructed with the average LDF shape.
With the LDF shape fixed by the parametrization of $\beta$, the shower size $S(300)$ and the core position $\vec{x}_{\mathrm{c}}$ are determined simultaneously through a maximum-likelihood fit.
The likelihood accounts for the measured signals in the triggered stations and for active stations that did not trigger.
Although no signal trace is available for these non-triggered stations, their presence constrains the fit, since an LDF predicting a signal above the trigger threshold at their positions is disfavoured.
In this way, the LDF fit determines $S(300)$ and refines the core position obtained from the initial geometrical reconstruction.

\section{Correction for zenith-angle attenuation}
\label{sec:cic}

The reconstructed signal $S(300)$ provides an estimator of the shower size at ground and contains information about the energy and longitudinal development of the primary cosmic-ray shower.
However, at a fixed primary energy, $S(300)$ also depends on the zenith angle.
Inclined showers traverse a larger atmospheric slant depth before reaching the detector and are therefore observed at a later stage of their development than more vertical showers of the same energy.
Since the electromagnetic component attenuates more rapidly in the atmosphere than the more penetrating muonic component, their relative contributions to the signal at ground change with zenith angle.
This effect is coupled to the different responses of the water-Cherenkov detector to electromagnetic particles and muons.
Consequently, showers with the same primary energy but different zenith angles do not generally produce the same $S(300)$, and this dependence must be corrected before the shower size can be used as an energy estimator.
The correction is determined from data using the constant-intensity-cut method, first introduced by Hersil et al.~\cite{Hirsil:1961:CIC}.
The corrected shower size, $S_{30}$, is the value of $S(300)$ that the shower would have produced at the reference angle $\theta_{\text{ref}}=30^\circ$.
This angle is chosen as the median of the studied event distribution, reducing the typical magnitude of the correction.
The measured and corrected shower sizes are related through the attenuation function,
\begin{align}
    S(300)=S_{30}f_{\text{CIC}}(\theta,S_{30})
    \label{eq:attenuation}
\end{align}
which is normalized such that $f_{\text{CIC}}(30^\circ,S_{30})=1$.

\subsection{The Constant-intensity cut method}
\label{subsec:cic_method}
To determine the attenuation function, the \gls{cic} method is used.
The large-scale dipolar anisotropy reported by the Pierre Auger Observatory becomes significant above $8\,\text{EeV}$~\cite{PierreAuger:2017:LargeScaleAnisotropy}, while below this energy the flux is considered isotropic.
The events are binned in equal-width intervals of $\sin^2\theta$, which have equal exposure.
For the \sd{433}, the range $0^\circ\leq\theta<45^\circ$ is divided into nine such intervals.
Within each interval, events are ranked by decreasing $S(300)$.
Events at the same rank $N$ correspond approximately to the same primary energy, and the variation of their $S(300)$ with zenith angle determines the attenuation curve at that intensity.
Six intensity cuts, $N\in\{100,\,200,\,500,\,1000,\,2000,\,5000\}$, are used to determine the attenuation at different shower sizes.
The statistical uncertainty of $S_N$ is estimated from the Poisson fluctuations of $N$ as
\begin{align}
    \sigma_{S_N}
    =
    \frac{S_{N-\sqrt{N}}-S_{N+\sqrt{N}}}{2}.
\end{align}
The attenuation also depends on the shower size, since the depth of the shower maximum and the contributions of the electromagnetic and muonic components evolve with energy.
This dependence is included using $S_{30}$ as an energy estimator.
The fit uses $x=\sin^2\theta-\sin^2\theta_{\text{ref}}$ and $y=\lg(S_{30}/S_0)$, where the reference scale $S_0=40\,\text{VEM}$ is introduced to reduce correlations among the parameters.
The attenuation function is described by a third-degree polynomial in $x$, with coefficients that depend linearly on $y$,
\begin{align}
    f_{\text{CIC}}(\theta,S_{30})
    =
    1+(a_0+a_1y)x+(b_0+b_1y)x^2+c_0x^3.
\end{align}
First, each intensity is fitted independently.
The resulting parameters are then used as starting values for a simultaneous fit to all six intensities and nine zenith-angle bins.
The global fit gives $\chi^2/N_{\text{dof}}=31.80/43$.
The best fit parameters are shown in \cref{table:cic_parameters}.

Since $f_{\text{CIC}}$ depends on the unknown $S_{30}$, the correction is applied iteratively, starting from $S_{30}=S(300)$ and updating it as $S_{30}=S(300)/f_{\text{CIC}}(\theta,S_{30})$ until its relative change is smaller than $10^{-4}$.

The lower panel shows the corresponding attenuation curves, illustrating their dependence on $S_{30}$.
Compared with the attenuation curve at the reference size $S_{30}=40\,\mathrm{VEM}$, the shower-size dependence changes the reconstructed $S_{30}$ by less than $3.5\%$, with the largest effect occurring for vertical showers.

\section{Calibration of the SD-433 energy estimator}
\label{sec:energy_calibration}
\label{sec:calibration}

With the attenuation in the atmosphere corrected, the shower size $S_{30}$ can be calibrated in terms of the primary energy.
The other two surface-detector arrays, the \sd{750} and \sd{1500}, are calibrated directly using events detected simultaneously by the \gls{sd} and \gls{fd}.
However, because of the distance between the \sd{433} and the nearest \gls{fd} site, Cohihueco, the \gls{heat} telescopes cannot observe showers falling within the \sd{433} area.
The \sd{433} energy scale is therefore obtained through an \gls{sd}-\gls{sd} calibration using events detected in coincidence with the \sd{750}.
This introduces an additional complication because the \sd{750} calibration relation must be extrapolated by approximately two to three energy bins below the range in which that array is fully efficient, as discussed in \cref{sec:sd750_efficiency}.
The finite resolutions of both arrays, their correlation, and the event selection must consequently be included in the calibration fit.

\subsection{SD-750 reference scale and coincident events}
\label{subsec:calibration_sample}

The calibration requires events reconstructed with high quality by both arrays.
The standard spectrum-quality selections are therefore applied independently to the \sd{433} and \sd{750} datasets.
The events are required to have $\theta<40^\circ$, satisfy the 6T5 fiducial condition, and fall outside the periods of unstable acquisition.
The \sd{750} dataset is obtained from an updated Herald reconstruction of the data used in Ref.~\cite{PierreAuger:2021:InfillSpectrum}, restricted to the same acquisition period as the \sd{433} dataset.
Its energies are recalculated using the updated ICRC 2025 calibration based on the revised \gls{fd} reconstruction~\cite{Verzi:2021:EnergyScale,Verzi:2025:Calibration}.

The \sd{750} energy calibration is described by the power law
\begin{align}
    E^{\text{SD-750}}
    =
    A^{\text{SD-750}}
    \left(
        \frac{S^{\text{SD-750}}_{35}}{S_0^{\text{SD-750}}}
    \right)^{B^{\text{SD-750}}},
    \label{eq:sd750_cal}
\end{align}
where $S^{\text{SD-750}}_{35}$ is the corrected shower-size estimator, $A^{\text{SD-750}}$ sets the energy scale, and $B^{\text{SD-750}}$ is the slope.
The values applied in this work are $A^{\text{SD-750}}=13.2912\,\mathrm{PeV}$, $B^{\text{SD-750}}=0.9985676$, and $S_0^{\text{SD-750}}=1\,\mathrm{VEM}$.
After recalibrating the \sd{750} events, the two datasets are matched by their event identifiers, yielding \num{37505} coincident events.

\subsection{Likelihood fit and calibration relation}
\label{subsec:calibration_fit}

The relation between the \sd{433} shower size and the \sd{750} reference energy is also described by a power law,
\begin{align}
    E
    =
    A
    \left(
        \frac{S_{30}}{S_0}
    \right)^B,
    \label{eq:sd433_calibration}
\end{align}
where $S_0=40\,\mathrm{VEM}$ is chosen to be within the calibrated energy range to reduce the correlation between the fitted normalization $A$ and slope $B$.
The calibration is performed using the likelihood method introduced by Dembinski et al.~\cite{Dembinski:2015:Likelihood}.
The finite resolutions of both $S_{30}$ and the \sd{750} reference energy would bias a conventional $\chi^2$-fit and are therefore included in the likelihood.
Unlike an \gls{sd}-\gls{fd} calibration, the two measurements are correlated because both arrays observe the same shower and share some of the \gls{wcd}s.
For the \sd{750} resolution, the same parameterization from Ref.~\cite{PierreAuger:2021:InfillSpectrum} is adopted,
\begin{align}
    \frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{SD-750}}}{E}
    =
    \sqrt{
        \sigma_{\mathrm{sh}}^2
        +
        \left(\sigma_{\mathrm{det}}^{\mathrm{SD-750}}\right)^2
        \frac{E_0}{E}
    },
\end{align}
where $\sigma_{\mathrm{sh}}=0.09$ describes the energy-independent shower-to-shower fluctuations, $\sigma_{\mathrm{det}}^{\mathrm{SD-750}}=0.21$ parameterizes the detector contribution, and $E_0=100\,\mathrm{PeV}$.

Because both arrays sample the same shower, their shower-to-shower fluctuations are fully correlated.
An additional detector correlation arises from the \gls{wcd}s shared by the two arrays.
The $S_{30}$ resolution is estimated through the inverse calibration using an initial slope $B_0=1$,
\begin{align}
    \frac{\sigma_{S_{30}}}{S_{30}}
    =
    \frac{1}{B_0}
    \sqrt{
        \sigma_{\mathrm{sh}}^2
        +
        \left(\sigma_{\mathrm{det}}^{\mathrm{SD-433}}\right)^2
        \frac{E_0}{E}
    },
\end{align}
where $\sigma_{\mathrm{det}}^{\mathrm{SD-433}}=0.12$.
The combined response function is then modelled as a correlated two-dimensional Gaussian.
An underlying shower size $s$ is therefore introduced, allowing the reconstructed $S_{30}$ to fluctuate around it according to the \sd{433} resolution model.
For a given $s$, the calibration relation predicts the energy $E_{\mathrm{cal}}(s\mid A,B)=A(s/S_0)^B$.
The same reasoning is also applied to the \sd{750} energy, allowing the reconstructed $E_i^{\mathrm{SD-750}}$ to fluctuate around $E_{\mathrm{cal}}(s\mid A,B)$ according to the \sd{750} resolution model.
The measured pair $(S_{30,i},E_i^{\mathrm{SD-750}})$ is therefore described by a correlated two-dimensional Gaussian.

The corresponding response function for event $i$ is
\begin{align}
    R_i
    \left(
        S_{30,i},
        E_i^{\mathrm{SD-750}}
        \mid
        s,A,B
    \right)
    =
    \frac{
        \exp\left[-Q_i(s\mid A,B)/2\right]
    }{
        2\pi
        \sigma_{S,i}
        \sigma_{E,i}
        \sqrt{1-\rho_i^2}
    },
    \label{eq:calibration_response}
\end{align}
where $\sigma_{S,i}$ and $\sigma_{E,i}$ are the absolute uncertainties of $S_{30,i}$ and $E_i^{\mathrm{SD-750}}$, respectively.
The energy-dependent correlation coefficient is calculated as
\begin{align}
    \rho_i
    =
    \frac{
        \displaystyle
        \frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{sh}}^2}{B_0}
        +
        \rho_{\mathrm{det}}
        \sigma_{\mathrm{det}}^{\mathrm{SD-433}}
        \sigma_{\mathrm{det}}^{\mathrm{SD-750}}
        \frac{E_0}{E_i^{\mathrm{SD-750}}}
    }{
        \displaystyle
        \left(
            \frac{\sigma_{S,i}}{S_{30,i}}
        \right)
        \left(
            \frac{\sigma_{E,i}}{E_i^{\mathrm{SD-750}}}
        \right)
    },
\end{align}
where $\rho_{\mathrm{det}}=0.33$ is the assumed correlation between the detector contributions arising from the \gls{wcd}s shared by the two arrays.
The quantity $Q_i$ is the quadratic correlated distance between the measured pair and the corresponding point on the calibration curve,
\begin{align}
    Q_i(s\mid A,B)
    =
    \frac{1}{1-\rho_i^2}
    \Bigg[
        &
        \left(
            \frac{S_{30,i}-s}{\sigma_{S,i}}
        \right)^2
        +
        \left(
            \frac{
                E_i^{\mathrm{SD-750}}
                -
                E_{\mathrm{cal}}(s\mid A,B)
            }{\sigma_{E,i}}
        \right)^2
        \nonumber\\
        &
        -
        2\rho_i
        \left(
            \frac{S_{30,i}-s}{\sigma_{S,i}}
        \right)
        \left(
            \frac{
                E_i^{\mathrm{SD-750}}
                -
                E_{\mathrm{cal}}(s\mid A,B)
            }{\sigma_{E,i}}
        \right)
    \Bigg].
    \label{eq:calibration_mahalanobis}
\end{align}

Because the event distribution falls steeply with shower size, upward migrations are more frequent than downward migrations.
The distribution of the shower size is therefore included in the likelihood.
An empirical distribution is used, defined as
\begin{align}
    h(s)=\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{evt}}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N_{\mathrm{evt}}}\delta(s-S_{30,j})
\end{align}
where the sum is done over the observed events, $N_{\mathrm{evt}}$ is the total number of events and $\delta$ is Dirac's delta function.
When binning in $\lg(S_{30}/\mathrm{\,VEM})$ this expression can be simplified to $w_k=n_k/N_{\mathrm{evt}}$, where $n_k$ is the number of events in bin $k$.
With this, the single event likelihood can be defined
\begin{align}
    \mathcal{L}_i(A,B)=\sum\limits_k w_k R_i
    \left(
        S_{30,i},
        E_i^{\mathrm{SD-750}}
        \mid
        s_k,A,B
    \right),
\end{align}
where the sum over $k$ replaces the integral due to the binning in $\lg(s_k/\mathrm{VEM})$.
Finally, the combined log-likelihood is written as $\ln\mathcal{L}(A,B)=\sum\limits_i\ln\mathcal{L}_i(A,B)$.
Since the \sd{433} has a better resolution, the event selection is applied to $S_{30}$ to minimize migration effects.
The cut $S_{30}>30\,\mathrm{VEM}$ roughly corresponds to the full-efficiency threshold of the \sd{750}, leaving \num{5980} available events for the calibration.
However, events below the cut are still used to determine $h(s)$ and account for migrations across the threshold.
The fit results in $A=(149.49\pm0.34)\,\mathrm{PeV}$ and $B=0.963\pm0.002$, with a correlation $\rho_{AB}=-0.51$.
The calibration threshold $S_{30}=30\,\mathrm{VEM}$ corresponds to an energy of $113.3\,\mathrm{PeV}$.
The systematic uncertainty associated with the choice of the calibration threshold is evaluated by repeating the fit for $S_{30,\min}$ between $15$ and $40\,\mathrm{VEM}$.
As the threshold increases, the normalization $A$ decreases while the slope $B$ increases, resulting in strongly anticorrelated variations.
The full excursion of the fitted parameters is assigned as the systematic uncertainty, yielding $\Delta A_{\mathrm{syst}}=4.0\,\mathrm{PeV}$ and $\Delta B_{\mathrm{syst}}=0.035$.
The fit with $S_{30,\min}=15\,\mathrm{VEM}$ extends well below the nominal \sd{750} efficiency range and approximately down to the full-efficiency threshold of the \sd{433}, therefore providing a conservative estimate of the systematic envelope.

Although individual residuals can reach approximately $20\%$, above the calibration threshold the mean residual remains close to zero and decreases quickly with energy.

Finally, the energy distribution of the \sd{433} events is shown in the right panel of \cref{fig:sd433_energy_calibration_residuals}, together with the full-efficiency threshold.
Below this threshold, the detection efficiency increases with energy, producing the rising event distribution despite the decreasing cosmic ray flux.

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:energy_reconstruction_summary}

In this chapter we present the energy reconstruction of the events recorded with the \sd{433}.
After reconstructing the shower geometry, $S(300)$ is obtained by modelling the decrease of the signal with the distance to the shower core.
From the reconstructed $S(300)$, the attenuation function is determined to correct for the different zenith angles of the showers.
The zenith-independent shower size $S_{30}$ is then obtained by iteratively applying the shower-size-dependent correction $f_{\mathrm{CIC}}(\theta,S_{30})$.
Using the events detected in common with the \sd{750} and the zenith-independent shower size estimator we then developed the energy calibration for the \sd{433}.
The likelihood formalism was introduced to take into account the finite resolution both of \sd{433} in the shower size and \sd{750} in energy.
An empirical shower-size distribution is included to account for the preferential migration from low to high energies produced by the steep cosmic ray spectrum.
Minimizing the log-likelihood yields $A=(149.49\pm0.34)\,\mathrm{PeV}$ and $B=0.963\pm0.002$.
The mean difference between the calibrated \sd{433} energy and the \sd{750} reference energy remains below $3\%$ throughout the fitted energy range and decreases with increasing energy.
Finally, the calibrated \sd{433} energy distribution forms the basis of the cosmic ray spectrum measurement presented in the next chapter.
