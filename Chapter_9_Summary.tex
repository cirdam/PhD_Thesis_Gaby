This thesis investigated the second knee in the all-particle cosmic-ray energy spectrum using the low-energy extensions of the Pierre Auger Observatory surface detector. The analysis established a spectrum measurement with the SD-433 array and broadened its energy reach by combining it with the SD-750 data. Together, these measurements characterize the spectral softening in an energy range that is key to interpreting the decline of the Galactic cosmic-ray component and the possible emergence of an extragalactic contribution.

The SD-433 measurement required a consistent treatment of the dataset, energy reconstruction, and detector response. Studies of the array’s operating conditions, detection efficiency, and stability defined a dataset suitable for measuring the spectrum above \(10^{16.8}\,\mathrm{eV}\) at zenith angles below \(40^\circ\). The exposure was derived from the time-dependent active configuration after excluding periods of unstable data acquisition. Together, these steps provided the basis for converting the observed eventsinto a cosmic-ray flux measurement.

The energy reconstruction connected the measured shower size to the energy scale of the Observatory. A constant-intensity correction dependent on the size of the shower accounted for the attenuation of the surface signal with the zenith angle. 
Events recorded by both the \sd{433} and \sd{750} allowed the corrected SD-433 shower size to be calibrated against the SD-750 energy.
The calibration likelihood accounted for the finite resolution of both observables and the effect of the steeply falling event distribution. This procedure inherited the energy scale from the \gls{fd} to the \sd{433} measurement.

For the spectrum measurement, detector effects were incorporated through a forward-folding of the observed energy distribution during the fit. 
The response matrix incorporated the detection efficiency, energy bias, and resolution, allowing the spectral parameters to be inferred from the reconstructed events.
The approximation used to remove the dependence of the response matrix to the cosmic-ray flux when constructing the matrix was validated pior to its use on data. 
The resulting framework also provided the correction factors used to obtain the unfolded spectrum.

The SD-433 spectrum exhibits a softening that is described by a broken power law with a smooth transition. 
The second knee is found at $E_{01}=(2.8\pm0.5_{\mathrm{stat}}\pm0.7_{\mathrm{syst}})\times10^{17}\,\mathrm{eV}.$
The spectral index changes from \(3.04\pm0.02_{\mathrm{stat}}\pm0.09_{\mathrm{syst}}\) below the break to \(3.34\pm0.08_{\mathrm{stat}}\pm0.11_{\mathrm{syst}}\) above it. The available SD-433 statistics do not independently constrain the transition width. The systematic study identifies the inherited fluorescence-detector energy scale as the dominant contribution to the uncertainty in the break energy and flux normalization, while the calibration uncertainty has the strongest effect on the spectral indices.

An updated SD-750 spectrum extends the energy range of the spectrum through the ankle. Its detection efficiency was re-determined using events recorded in common with the\sd{433}, supporting a full efficiency threshold of $\lg(E/\text{eV})=17.2$.
Combining the two spectra provides greater sensitivity above the second knee and to the width of the transition.
The preliminary result of the combined fit yields a second-knee energy of \(170\pm35_{\mathrm{stat}}\pm24_{\mathrm{syst}}\,\mathrm{PeV}\) and a transition-width parameter of \(\omega_{01}=0.31\pm0.11_{\mathrm{stat}}\pm0.09_{\mathrm{syst}}\).

These combined results need further study. The second-knee fitted energy lies near the lower edge of the SD-750 range and shifts to higher energy when the first SD-750 bin is excluded. Although the fitted spectral indices are consistent with the standalone SD-433 measurement, the spectral mismatch near the overlap boundary and the corresponding displacement of the break are not yet understood. Clarifying this behavior is necessary to establish the robustness of the combined determination of the second-knee position and transition width.

Comparison with other experiments places the measured break within the range of previously reported second-knee energies. Aligning the spectra at their respective break energies reveals a consistent softening. This comparison supports a common qualitative picture, but does not establish the origin of the differences among the measurements.

It is also noteworthy that the all-particle spectrum alone cannot determine the astrophysical origin of the second knee. Its position and shape must be interpreted together with measurements sensitive to primary mass and arrival direction. Muon measurements with the Underground Muon Detector of the Pierre Auger Observatory and complementary observations of shower maximum can help determine whether the softening is accompanied by a change in composition. Combined with anisotropy studies, these observations can test scenarios involving rigidity-dependent Galactic cutoffs, additional Galactic populations, and an emerging extragalactic component.

The central result of this thesis is a measurement of the cosmic-ray energy spectrum with the SD-433 array of the Pierre Auger Observatory. It quantifies the spectral softening associated with the second knee by determining the break position and the spectral indices on either side, including statistical and systematic uncertainties. This result relies in a detailed characterization of the dataset, the energy calibration, and the modeling of the detector response.
An ongoing extension combines the SD-433 spectrum with the SD-750 measurement to extend the covered energy range and constrain the second-knee transition width.
Looking ahead, the combination of the spectra from all three surface arrays could deliver a single, cross-calibrated measurement spanning more than four decades in energy.
