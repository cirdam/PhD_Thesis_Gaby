\chapter{Comparison with other experiments}
\label{chapter:comparison}

In this chapter the standalone \sd{433} measurement and the preliminary combined \sd{433}--\sd{750} spectrum with earlier results from the Pierre Auger Observatory and other experiments are compared.
In the \sd{433} and combined spectra, the spectral indices below and above the second knee are compatible, while the fitted knee energy differs, as discussed in Chapter~7.
Thanks to its broad energy coverage, the combined spectrum enables the comparison to be extended beyond the second-knee transition and through the ankle.

% Comparison with other Auger measurements
In \cref{fig:comparison}, we present the combined spectrum and the standalone \sd{433} result together with earlier measurements.
These include the preliminary \sd{433} spectrum reported at the International Cosmic Ray Conference (ICRC) 2023~\cite{BrichettoOrquera:2023}, which differs from the present analysis in the \Offline reconstruction version, the procedure used to flag bad data-taking periods, and the adopted \gls{fd} energy scale.
The resulting discrepancy between the spectra is seen predominantly as an approximately vertical offset, attributable to the modified energy scale.
In the ICRC 2023 \sd{433} analysis, the second knee was found at $E_{01}=(230\pm50\,\mathrm{(stat)}\pm35\,\mathrm{(syst)})\,\mathrm{PeV}$, which is compatible within the stated uncertainties with both the current standalone \sd{433} and the combined measurements.
The spectral indices below and above the knee are likewise consistent with those obtained in this study.

The combined Cherenkov and SD spectrum reported in \cite{Novotny:2021:CherenkovSpectrum}, and reproduced in \cref{fig:comparison}, reaches lower energies by using the fluorescence telescopes to observe direct atmospheric Cherenkov light from air showers arriving in their direction.
This approach reduces the energy threshold, but the determination of exposure and the energy calibration is more complex than for surface-detector measurements.
The quoted second-knee energy, $158\pm5\,\mathrm{(stat)}\pm20\,\mathrm{(syst)}\,\mathrm{PeV}$, is below the standalone \sd{433} value and is close to the preliminary estimate from the combined spectrum.
The spectral index below the knee, $\gamma_0=2.85\pm0.01\,\mathrm{(stat)}\pm0.05\,\mathrm{(syst)}$, is likewise smaller than the indices obtained in this work.
% Differences in the lower energy boundary and the treatment of the \sd{750} efficiency may affect this comparison.
For the spectrum based on \sd{750} data published in 2021~\cite{PierreAuger:2021:InfillSpectrum}, the second-knee location was not fit freely but instead was fixed to a value derived using the \gls{fd}, and therefore it is not suitable for direct comparison with our present result. 
The \sd{433} is the first surface-detector array that can measure the second-knee energy.
% analysis done with the \sd{750}, the inferred spectral index below the knee is particularly low, although it is not directly constrained by the measured energy range and was subsequently held fixed when estimating the uncertainties of the remaining spectral parameters.

To facilitate a comparison of the spectral shapes in the vicinity of the second knee, each spectrum is shifted along the energy axis by a factor determined from its published second-knee energy,
\begin{align}
    E'=E\frac{E_{01}^{\mathrm{comb}}}{E_{01}^{\mathrm{exp}}},
\end{align}
where $E_{01}^{\mathrm{exp}}$ denotes the second-knee energy reported by a given experiment and $E_{01}^{\mathrm{comb}}$ is the corresponding value obtained from the combined spectrum presented in this work.
The combined spectrum is taken as the reference rather than the \sd{433} measurement, since its larger energy range allows a more accurate estimate of the spectral index above the knee.
In this straightforward rescaling, the vertical coordinates of the data points were kept fixed.
The outcome of this procedure is shown in \cref{fig:comparison_shift}.
Following the alignment, the spectra exhibit generally consistent slopes above the second knee and flux levels that agree within systematic uncertainties.
This resemblance in spectral shape indicates that differences in the absolute energy-scale normalization may explain part of the dispersion in the reported knee energies.

\subsection{Interpretation of the second knee measurement}

The joint fit prefers a wide second-knee transition, yielding $\omega_{01}=0.31\pm0.11\,\mathrm{(stat)}\pm0.09\,\mathrm{(syst)}$.
A similarly broad transition was noted in the published \sd{750} analysis, which reported $\omega_{01}=0.49\pm0.07\,\mathrm{(stat)}\pm0.34\,\mathrm{(syst)}$ when the knee energy and the spectral index below it were fixed.
Thus, both fits indicate a smooth steepening, but the extracted width varies with the constraints imposed on the spectrum below the knee.

In a basic Peters-cycle picture, the spectra of individual elements become steeper at energies that scale with charge, $E_Z\simeq Z E_{\mathrm{p}}$, which would put the iron knee at roughly $0.08$--$0.10\,\mathrm{EeV}$~\cite{Mollerach:2019:GalacticScenario,Aloisio:2012:Transition}.
The preliminary fit to the combined data locates the second knee at $0.17\,\mathrm{EeV}$, i.e., above that interval, whereas the standalone \sd{433} measurement yields $0.28\,\mathrm{EeV}$, even further above.
Still, neither outcome alone is enough to rule out a rigidity-dependent Galactic explanation.
A different reading of the smooth steepening is that it reflects the overlap of a declining Galactic contribution with a rising extragalactic one~\cite{Aloisio:2012:Transition,Mollerach:2019:GalacticScenario,Lemoine:2005:MagneticHorizon}.
Before drawing stronger inferences from the fitted break energy and transition width, it is also necessary to clarify why the standalone \sd{433} and combined spectra differ in the vicinity of the second knee.

Muon measurements using the Pierre Auger Observatory’s Underground Muon Detector can help clarify whether the mass composition varies across the second knee, in conjunction with observations of $X_{\max}$ and how it changes with energy. Combined with anisotropy analyses, these data are essential for discriminating among a rigidity-dependent cutoff, an extra Galactic component, and the emergence of an extragalactic contribution~\cite{Thoudam:2016:SecondGalacticComponent,Giacinti:2012:AnisotropyTransition}.

\section{Summary}

Comparison with previous measurements places the results of this work in the broader experimental picture of the second knee.
Both the standalone \sd{433} and preliminary combined second-knee energies lie within the range reported by previous experiments.
Aligning the spectra at their fitted knee energies reveals broadly similar slopes above the transition.
Although the standalone and combined results also agree in their spectral indices, the difference between their spectra near the knee and the displacement of the fitted break remain unresolved.
Understanding these differences, together with measurements of mass composition and anisotropy, is needed to clarify the physical origin of the second knee.
