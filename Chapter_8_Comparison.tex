\chapter{Comparison with other experiment}
\label{chapter:comparison}

With the measurement developed from the \sd{433} data, the update of the \sd{750} spectrum and further the combination between the two measurements, the second knee could finally be measured.
The \sd{433} measurement of the second knee is constrained by the fewer statistics in the dataset, the width of the transition cannot be asserted.
On the other hand, the \sd{750} measurement has a much higher statistical weight but the full efficiency is reached $\lg(E/\mathrm{eV})=17.2$ and the position of the break nor the spectral index before the second knee cannot be measured.
Combining the strong points of the two measurements, the combined spectrum can be finally be developed and the second knee properly characterized in the fit.
There is good agreement between the two spectral indices before and after the second knee between the \sd{433} and the combined spectrum.
In \cref{fig:comparison} the measured spectra in this work are shown together with previous measurements with other spectra.
The diamond show the estimated flux from the combined spectrum and the circles represent the \sd{433} alone measurements.
A preliminary measurement with the \sd{433} from ICRC 2023 \cite{BrichettoOrquera:2023} is also shown for reference.
The main difference between the two \sd{433} measurement is the updated \Offline reconstruction, improved bad period estimation and the updated \gls{fd} energy scale and is seen as an almost vertical shift in the spectrum due to the change in the exposure.
This measurement is estimated with events from $0-45^\circ$ and using events where the silent station flags had to be repaired, as previously mentioned in \cref{sec:sd433_dataset_summary}.
The change in the dataset is, however, less than $10\%$ overall, but is compensated with the appropriate exposure correction.
The second knee is measured at $E_{01} = (230 ± 50 \text{ stat} ± 35 \text{ syst})\,\text{PeV}$. 
This value is consistent with both the \sd{433} and combined spectrum measurements.
There is also good agreement between the $\gamma_0$ and $\gamma_1$ indices.

Combined measurements from \cite{PierreAuger:2021:Combined} shows the combined \gls{sd} and the Cherenkov spectrum measured with the \gls{fd} shown in black squares.
In this measurement, Cherenkov light produce by the showers in the atmosphere is detected directly for events pointing to the telescopes.
This allows for the threshold energy to be greatly reduced, but at the expense of a harder to determine exposure and energy calibration when compared with straightforward SD-SD calibration.
For this measurement, the position of the second knee is in some tension with the \sd{433} measurement. 
However, it agrees with the combined measurement.
This is expected since the \sd{750} spectrum dominates the exposure at the energies around the second knee.
The reported $\gamma_0$ is lower than the measured with this work but it can be due to the used minimum energy of the \sd{750} events which acording to this work has an unacounted efficiency effect.

The other measurements shown in \cref{fig:comparison} shows the results for the all-particle spectrum from other experiments.
The markers in color are used for experiments with a calorimetric energy scale from fluorescence detectors and the gray datapoints are used for experiments calibrated via simulations.
Most of the measurements are contained within the systematic flux band from the combined spectrum and all the spectra shows the presence of the change in the slope of the spectum at different energies.
The range for $\gamma_0$ is from around 2.8 to 3.8 while for $\gamma_1$ the measurement ranges between 3.2 and 3.37.
The reported results for $E_{01}$, $\gamma_0$ and $\gamma1$ are shown in \cref{table:second_knee} for the different experiments.
