\chapter{Comparison with other experiment}
\label{chapter:comparison}

With the measurement developed from the \sd{433} data, the update of the \sd{750} spectrum and further the combination between the two measurements, the second knee could finally be measured.
The \sd{433} measurement of the second knee is constrained by the fewer statistics in the dataset, the width of the transition cannot be asserted.
On the other hand, the \sd{750} measurement has a much higher statistical weight but the full efficiency is reached $\lg(E/\mathrm{eV})=17.2$ and the position of the break nor the spectral index before the second knee cannot be measured.
Combining the strong points of the two measurements, the combined spectrum can be finally be developed and the second knee properly characterized in the fit.
There is good agreement between the two spectral indices before and after the second knee between the \sd{433} and the combined spectrum.
In \cref{fig:comparison} the measured spectra in this work are shown together with previous measurements with other spectra.
The diamond show the estimated flux from the combined spectrum and the circles represent the \sd{433} alone measurements.
A preliminary measurement with the \sd{433} from ICRC 2023 \cite{BrichettoOrquera:2023} is also shown for reference.
The main difference between the two \sd{433} measurement is the updated \Offline reconstruction, improved bad period estimation and the updated \gls{fd} energy scale and is seen as an almost vertical shift in the spectrum due to the change in the exposure.
This measurement is estimated with events from $0-45^\circ$ and using events where the silent station flags had to be repaired, as previously mentioned in \cref{sec:sd433_dataset_summary}.
The change in the dataset is, however, less than $10\%$ overall, but is compensated with the appropriate exposure correction.
The second knee is measured at $E_{01}=(230\pm50\,\mathrm{(stat)}\pm35\,\mathrm{(syst)})\,\mathrm{PeV}$.
This value is consistent with both the \sd{433} and combined spectrum measurements.
There is also good agreement between the $\gamma_0$ and $\gamma_1$ indices.


Combined measurements from \cite{Novotny:2021:CherenkovSpectrum} shows the combined \gls{sd} and the Cherenkov spectrum measured with the \gls{fd} shown in black squares.
In this measurement, Cherenkov light produced by the showers in the atmosphere is detected directly for events pointing to the telescopes.
This allows for the threshold energy to be greatly reduced, but at the expense of a harder to determine exposure and energy calibration when compared with straightforward SD-SD calibration.
For this measurement, the position of the second knee is in some tension with the \sd{433} measurement.
However, it agrees with the combined measurement.
This is expected since the \sd{750} spectrum dominates the exposure at the energies around the second knee.
The reported $\gamma_0$ is lower than the measured with this work but it can be due to the used minimum energy of the \sd{750} events which according to this work has an unaccounted efficiency effect.

The other measurements shown in \cref{fig:comparison} shows the results for the all-particle spectrum from other experiments.
The markers in color are used for experiments with a calorimetric energy scale from fluorescence detectors and the gray data points are used for experiments calibrated via simulations.
Most of the measurements are contained within the systematic flux band from the combined spectrum and all the spectra shows the presence of the change in the slope of the spectrum at different energies.
The range for $\gamma_0$ is from around 2.8 to 3.8 while for $\gamma_1$ the measurement ranges between 3.2 and 3.37.
The reported results for $E_{01}$, $\gamma_0$ and $\gamma_1$ are shown in \cref{tab:second_knee_comparison} for the different experiments.

In \cref{fig:comparison_shift}, the results from experiments that report a measurement of the second knee are shifted to match all the second knees in the energy of the second knee from the combined spectrum via a transformation,
\begin{align}
    E'=E\frac{E_{01}^\mathrm{comb}}{E_{01}^\mathrm{exp}}
\end{align}
\noindent where $E^\mathrm{x}_{01}$ is the measured second knee energy, to preserve the binning in logarithmic scale.
The behavior of all the experiments is quite consistent for higher energies, where a softening of the spectrum roll over and the slopes are quite consistent.
This hints that the primary difference in the measured second knee energies and the all particle flux between the different spectra arises from the differences in the energy scale rather than from a difference in the shape of the spectrum.
Experiments with the biggest deviation are the ones where the energy scale is not set calorimetrically, like KASCADE-Grande
Measurements such as TALE and Yakutsk, for instance, are almost completely contained within the systematic uncertainty band from the combined spectrum in the overlapping region.
It is noteworthy that all measurments show a smooth transition that last several bins, however this is the first to characterize the width of the transition, highlighting that the second knee is a soft change in the slope of the all particle spectrum.

The second-knee parameters reported by the experiments that locate the feature are summarized in \cref{tab:second_knee_comparison}.
For entries with two uncertainties, the first is statistical and the second systematic.
A single uncertainty denotes the fit uncertainty when the publication does not provide a separate systematic contribution.
A hyphen indicates that the parameter was not reported, while parameters held constant in the fit are marked as fixed.
The TA result presented at ICRC 2019 is not listed separately because its second-knee value is taken from the same TALE measurement shown in the table.
