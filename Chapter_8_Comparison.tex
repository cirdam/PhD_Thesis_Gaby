\chapter{Comparison with other experiments}
\label{chapter:comparison}

This chapter compares the standalone \sd{433} measurement and the preliminary combined \sd{433}--\sd{750} spectrum with previous measurements from the Pierre Auger Observatory and other experiments.
Both results give compatible spectral indices below and above the second knee, but differ in the fitted knee position.
As discussed in Chapter 7, this displacement remains unresolved.
With its wider energy coverage, the combined spectrum allows the comparison to extend above the second-knee transition and through the ankle.

In \cref{fig:comparison}, the combined spectrum and the standalone \sd{433} measurement are shown with diamonds and circles, respectively, alongside previous measurements.
Among these is the preliminary \sd{433} spectrum presented at ICRC 2023~\cite{BrichettoOrquera:2023}, which differs from the current analysis in the \Offline reconstruction, the identification of bad acquisition periods and the \gls{fd} energy scale.
The difference between the two spectra appears mainly as an almost vertical shift, associated with the change in exposure.
The ICRC 2023 analysis placed the second knee at $E_{01}=(230\pm50\,\mathrm{(stat)}\pm35\,\mathrm{(syst)})\,\mathrm{PeV}$, consistent within the quoted uncertainties with both the current standalone \sd{433} and combined results.
Its spectral indices below and above the knee also agree with those obtained in this work.

Black squares show the combined Cherenkov and SD spectrum presented in \cite{Novotny:2021:CherenkovSpectrum}, which extends to lower energies by using the fluorescence telescopes to detect direct atmospheric Cherenkov light from showers directed toward them.
This lowers the detection threshold, although determining the exposure and energy calibration is more involved than for the surface-detector measurements.
The reported second-knee energy, $158\pm5\,\mathrm{(stat)}\pm20\,\mathrm{(syst)}\,\mathrm{PeV}$, is lower than the standalone \sd{433} result and close to the preliminary combined value.
Its spectral index below the knee, $\gamma_0=2.85\pm0.01\,\mathrm{(stat)}\pm0.05\,\mathrm{(syst)}$, is also lower than those obtained in this work.
Differences in the lower energy boundary and the treatment of the \sd{750} efficiency may affect this comparison.
In the EPJC analysis, the inferred index below the knee is particularly low, although it is not directly constrained by the measured energy range and was subsequently held fixed when estimating the uncertainties of the remaining spectral parameters.

Measurements from other experiments are also shown in \cref{fig:comparison}, with colored markers indicating calorimetric energy scales and gray markers indicating calibrations based on simulations.
These spectra show a softening at different energies, while the reported indices above the break are generally closer to one another.
The second-knee energies and spectral parameters are collected in \cref{tab:second_knee_comparison}.

To compare the spectral shapes around the second knee, the spectra in \cref{fig:comparison_shift} are shifted along the energy axis according to
\begin{align}
    E'=E\frac{E_{01}^{\mathrm{comb}}}{E_{01}^{\mathrm{exp}}},
\end{align}
where $E_{01}^{\mathrm{exp}}$ is the second-knee energy reported by each experiment and $E_{01}^{\mathrm{comb}}$ is the preliminary combined value.
The combined spectrum is used as the reference because its wider energy coverage provides a more precise determination of the spectral index above the knee than the standalone \sd{433} measurement.
This transformation preserves the logarithmic bin widths and leaves the flux values unchanged, placing each fitted knee at the same energy.

After alignment, the spectra show broadly similar slopes above the second knee.
TALE and Yakutsk lie largely within the systematic uncertainty band of the combined spectrum over the overlapping energy range, while larger differences remain for KASCADE-Grande.
The similarity of the spectral shapes suggests that differences in energy scale may account for part of the spread in the reported knee energies.

The combined fit favors a broad second-knee transition, with $\omega_{01}=0.31\pm0.11\,\mathrm{(stat)}\pm0.09\,\mathrm{(syst)}$.
A broad transition was also reported in the published \sd{750} analysis, which gave $\omega_{01}=0.49\pm0.07\,\mathrm{(stat)}\pm0.34\,\mathrm{(syst)}$ with the knee energy and the index below it held fixed.
Both fits therefore describe a gradual steepening, although the fitted width depends on how the spectrum below the knee is constrained.

In a simple Peters-cycle scenario, individual elemental spectra steepen at energies proportional to their charge, $E_Z\simeq Z E_{\mathrm{p}}$, placing the iron knee at approximately $0.08$ to $0.10\,\mathrm{EeV}$~\cite{Mollerach:2019:GalacticScenario,Aloisio:2012:Transition}.
The preliminary combined fit places the second knee at $0.17\,\mathrm{EeV}$, above this range, while the standalone \sd{433} result at $0.28\,\mathrm{EeV}$ lies higher still.
Neither result, however, is sufficient on its own to exclude a rigidity-dependent Galactic origin.
An overlap between a fading Galactic component and an emerging extragalactic component offers another possible interpretation of the gradual steepening~\cite{Aloisio:2012:Transition,Mollerach:2019:GalacticScenario,Lemoine:2005:MagneticHorizon}.
Understanding the difference between the standalone \sd{433} and combined spectra near the second knee is also needed before drawing firmer conclusions from the fitted break energy and transition width.

Measurements of the muonic component with the Underground Muon Detector of the Pierre Auger Observatory could help establish whether the composition changes across the second knee, complemented by measurements of $X_{\max}$ and its evolution with energy.
Together with anisotropy studies, this information is needed to distinguish between a rigidity-dependent cutoff, an additional Galactic component and the onset of an extragalactic contribution~\cite{Thoudam:2016:SecondGalacticComponent,Giacinti:2012:AnisotropyTransition}.

\section{Summary}

Comparison with previous measurements places the results of this work in the broader experimental picture of the second knee.
Both the standalone \sd{433} and preliminary combined second-knee energies lie within the range reported by previous experiments.
Aligning the spectra at their fitted knee energies reveals broadly similar slopes above the transition.
Although the standalone and combined results also agree in their spectral indices, the difference between their spectra near the knee and the displacement of the fitted break remain unresolved.
Understanding these differences, together with measurements of mass composition and anisotropy, is needed to clarify the physical origin of the second knee.
